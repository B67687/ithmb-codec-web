01

Your files

The decoder runs in your browser, and the open-source ithmb-core(opens in a new tab) library reads the file on your device. The decoder uploads nothing, and the site keeps no copy of your files. It stores only your language and light/dark theme choices in this browser's localStorage. Those values are not sent to us.

02

What we don't do

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • The site sets no cookies of its own, and there are no third-party trackers.
  • No analytics scripts and no ads.
  • The decoder does no uploads or file scanning.
  • We don't sell your data or share it for advertising.

Found a decoding problem? Open a GitHub issue(opens in a new tab) with a description and, if you can, a minimal synthetic reproduction. Don't attach personal photos or your own .ithmb files to a public issue: issues are public and searchable. To share a file privately, use the private advisory form(opens in a new tab).

03

Who runs this site

This site is run by Nami, a solo developer (not a company).

For site or decoding questions, open an issue in the codec repository(opens in a new tab). For security or privacy reports, use its private advisory form(opens in a new tab), not a public issue. For a privacy request, put "privacy" in the advisory title; that route is private. If you can't use GitHub, open a public issue without personal details and ask for another route. For this site, those are the only two contact routes.

We don't keep a separate copy of support messages. GitHub stores issues and advisories under its own terms: public issues stay public, and advisories may be retained after they are resolved. Please don't put personal data in a public issue.

04

Hosting and legal basis

Cloudflare Pages hosts this site, and Cloudflare, Inc.(opens in a new tab) processes the basic request data.

Cloudflare sees your IP address, the URL, your browser/User-Agent details and the time. It uses these to deliver pages, keep the site secure, and diagnose delivery errors.

Where EU or UK law applies, our legal basis is our legitimate interest in running this free public site (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR; the equivalent UK GDPR provision). You can object to that processing at any time — see "Your rights". We don't use consent, so there is no consent banner.

Cloudflare may also collect network error reports (NEL). We don't receive or use them to identify you.

Cloudflare, Inc. is based in the US, so request data may be processed outside the EEA or UK. Where required, Cloudflare's data processing addendum, including the EU Standard Contractual Clauses and UK Addendum, covers those transfers. GitHub handles issue and advisory content under its own terms.

We add no analytics, ads or tracking scripts. Cloudflare provides us with aggregate hosting metrics and may set a strictly necessary security cookie if it challenges a request. See Cloudflare's privacy policy(opens in a new tab).

05

How long data is kept

  • We keep no application data or application logs: no uploaded files, decoded images or accounts.
  • Cloudflare keeps hosting request data under its own retention periods; see Cloudflare's privacy policy (linked above). We don't copy that data into our own systems.
  • Language and theme settings stay in localStorage until you clear site data or your browser removes it. They are never sent to us.
06

Your rights

In the European Economic Area (EEA), the UK or similar jurisdictions, you can request access, correction, erasure, restriction, objection and portability. To exercise these rights, including the right to object, use the contact routes in section 03.

We don't keep a visitor database, an analytics profile or a copy of your files. For Cloudflare-held data, we don't hold a searchable copy of its request logs, so we may not be able to identify you from a request. We'll still respond and explain what we can and can't do.

You can complain to us, or directly to your local supervisory authority (the ICO in the UK).

We don't sell your data, share it for advertising, or build a profile of you. Cloudflare may automatically challenge suspected bot traffic as part of site security.
